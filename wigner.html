Wigner's Friend

Your friend is sealed in a laboratory with a quantum coin. They flip it and read the result. From where you're standing, outside the door, what can you honestly say happened?

?
Nobody has flipped the coin yet. Both logbooks agree: nothing has happened.
Friend's account

Inside the lab

"I haven't flipped anything yet."

Wigner's account

Outside the door

"Nothing in that room has a definite state yet — including my friend."

Why this is a genuine paradox, not a trick: the Friend's logbook is not lying. Once they read the coin, they have a definite result — heads, say — and no ordinary sense in which they are still "undecided." But standard quantum mechanics says Wigner, who has no access to the lab, must describe the friend and the coin together as one unresolved system: friend-saw-heads and friend-saw-tails, superposed, until Wigner's own measurement (opening the door) resolves it. Both logbooks are correct by the rules each observer is using. Nobody has ever settled whose rules are the more fundamental ones — that disagreement is the measurement problem, sharpened until it points at a person instead of a cat.
