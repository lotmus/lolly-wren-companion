Maxwell's Demon

Two chambers, one gap, and a box of molecules moving at random speeds. Sort the fast ones right and the slow ones left, and you'll build a temperature difference from nothing at all — for as long as your memory holds out.

Click a molecule while it's near the gap in the middle wall to usher it through — red-hot (fast) molecules belong on the right, blue-cold (slow) ones on the left. Every sort you make is written to the demon's memory tape below.

Why the tape matters: sorting molecules by speed lowers the entropy of the gas — you've made the box more orderly for free, which the Second Law forbids. The escape clause is the demon itself: every sorting decision is a bit of information, and that information has to live somewhere (the tape above). Landauer's Principle says erasing one bit of memory costs at least kT ln 2 of heat, released back into the world. Fill the tape and you must pay to clear it before you can sort again — and that heat is never less than what your sorting saved. The demon doesn't break the Second Law. It just moves the bill to the filing cabinet.
